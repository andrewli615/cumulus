## Product question

The target is a useful, understandable wake decision within an earlier window, but only after the user's required minimum sleep opportunity is met. A planned opportunity does not prove actual sleep duration; measured time asleep requires validated sleep-onset and sleep/wake estimates. A separately tested latest-wake fallback handles missing, stale, or uncertain data. Sleep-stage classification is one candidate task. Qualify signals and their availability first, then decide whether wake-opportunity detection, sleep/wake estimation, or stages are supported. Keep alert delivery, fallback behavior, prediction quality, and waking outcomes separate. The intended benefit is lower sleep inertia; stage or cycle prediction is only an intermediate signal. See [the target experience](EXPERIENCE.md), [behavior requirements](BEHAVIOR.md), and [MVP exit evidence](BUILD_PLAN.md#personal-mvp-exit-evidence).

## Research lead: cycle-phase alignment

The owner identified Halas's [cycle-phase alignment framework](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400215225_Optimizing_Wake-Up_Time_Calculation_Through_Sleep_Cycle_Phase_Alignment_A_Mathematical_Framework). It proposes candidate wake windows from sleep-onset latency, changing cycle lengths and stage composition, and circadian progression. The available ResearchGate copy is dated December 2025 and gives a hypothetical worked example. Its page does not establish empirical validation or peer-reviewed publication; treat the formula as a research proposal until those are verified. The paper itself notes individual variability and that real-time sensing could improve on static estimates.

Use cycle alignment, if useful, as an explainable baseline or timing prior after the sleep-opportunity gate—not as Cumulus's validated rule. Do not hard-code a universal 90-minute cycle: a retrospective polysomnography study of 369 participants found a median cycle duration of 96 minutes, wide interindividual variation, and a non-normal distribution ([Cajochen et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37914631/)). Sleep inertia also depends on prior sleep loss and circadian timing; evidence for the independent effect of sleep stage at waking is mixed ([Hilditch and McHill](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6710480/)).

For a later comparison, keep at least three strategies distinct: a fixed latest-wake baseline, a cycle-window prior, and a qualified signal-informed policy. Compare them only when the minimum sleep opportunity is met; measure post-wake sleepiness and a consistent performance outcome, not just agreement with estimated or Apple-reported stages. Do not implement or claim a benefit from this framework before the input gates and outcome protocol are satisfied.

## Review of engineer proposal — October 14, 2026

**Assessment:** a solid research direction, but not an implementation-ready model specification. Adopt its explicit questions, missing-data analysis, simple baselines, uncertainty reporting, and controlled outcome testing. Keep its four questions distinct: (1) whether heart rate adds to movement for stage estimates; (2) whether sleep history and physiology predict a morning restfulness rating; (3) whether an adaptive window reduces sleep inertia without sacrificing sleep; and (4) how missing or irregular inputs affect coverage and confidence. A stage label, a morning quality rating, and immediate post-wake sleep inertia are different targets. A minimum planned sleep opportunity can be enforced, but it does not prove actual sleep duration or quantify sleep lost; use those claims only after validating the relevant estimate.

The proposal's candidate data and models need compatibility checks before selection. Apple's Watch sleep and vital features do not establish that a third-party app can read equivalent samples at a useful time; validate the exact HealthKit types, authorization, sample age, coverage, and Series 8/watchOS 26.6 behavior against [Apple's HealthKit types](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/data-types) and [watchOS 26 vitals guide](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/watch/apd15aa7ed96/26/watchos/26). [BIDSleep 1.0.1](https://physionet.org/content/bidsleep-dataset/1.0.1/) is a useful offline candidate (47 healthy participants, 253 nights), but uses Apple Watch data paired with Dreem 2-derived labels, not a full polysomnography reference; check its license and data contract before use. [WatchSleepNet](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v287/wang25a.html) uses beat intervals, which sparse BPM readings or an SDNN summary cannot substitute for. A small study predicting perceived sleep quality is relevant to proposal question 2, but does not establish prediction of sleep inertia or alarm benefit ([M2Sleep study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0305258)).

Keep the execution order evidence-led: finish the current foreground-feature check; measure input coverage, freshness, and missingness on the intended path; select one target and its outcome/reference; then compare a simple baseline with any compatible candidate outside the Watch app, using participant-separated and, for personalization, chronological evaluation. Keep Watch collection/inference separate from offline training and evaluation; define retention and deletion before storing personal signals, keep raw records out of Git, and do not add cloud sync without a demonstrated need. For stage claims, Apple stages alone are not independent ground truth. Only after a causal decision and latest-wake fallback are justified should Cumulus test waking outcomes. A bedroom study of light, sound, and temperature is useful precedent for outcome trials, not evidence that a Watch haptic policy works ([Campanella et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38534801/)).

The October 9 choice remains an alarm-first, Watch-only personal MVP. The proposal's historical summaries and morning ratings can be research tools; they do not silently replace or expand that MVP. Physical fixed-alarm acceptance is still pending despite the owner's current choice to defer those tests while exploring features. Keep the sleep-opportunity gate, independent alarm, and existing alarm criteria; record any future adaptive trial's planned sleep opportunity, actual sleep loss only when validly measurable, delivered/perceived alert, post-wake sleepiness, and consistent performance outcome separately.

## Evaluation rules

1. Separate sleep/wake detection, three- or four-stage classification, sleep-summary estimation, and alarm benefit. Define the task before comparing numbers.
2. Keep all nights from each participant within one data partition. Fit preprocessing and model selection on training/validation data, not the test set.
